Right-of-Way & Easement Clearing in Ocala, FL

Plenty of Marion County land is landlocked on paper only — the deeded easement is right there in the title work, running a quarter mile through somebody's pines to your parcel. Getting from a line on a survey to a road you can actually drive is right-of-way clearing: cutting, grubbing, and stabilizing a defined corridor for access, utilities, or drainage, at a consistent width, to a spec someone else may need to sign off on.

Corridor Work We Handle

  • Deeded access easements — clearing the legal corridor to a back parcel so you can reach your own land, common around Fort McCoy, Citra, and the flag lots off the US 301 and CR 318 corridors
  • New driveway and farm road corridors — cleared, grubbed, and built into a drivable base (see driveway installation)
  • Utility easements — corridors for power runs to new homesites, well and septic access, or co-op line extensions, cleared to the utility's width and overhead spec
  • Drainage easements and swale lines — vegetation out, flow line regraded so easements actually carry water in the summer wet season
  • Survey line cutting — clean sight lines so your surveyor can shoot boundaries on wooded acreage without hacking their own path at hourly rates
  • Firebreaks — bare-mineral-soil breaks around rural structures and property edges

The Legal Line Matters More Than the Tree Line

Right-of-way work has a legal edge that ordinary clearing doesn't: cut ten feet outside your easement and you've committed trespass and timber damage on a neighbor's land — Florida law can award multiple damages for wrongfully cut trees. So we start every corridor job from the recorded document and the survey, not from where the old ruts run. If the easement language is vague ("ingress and egress" with no stated width is common in older Marion County deeds), we'll tell you to resolve the width question first, because clearing is cheap compared to litigation.

One more local wrinkle: work inside a county road right-of-way — driveway connections, culvert sets on a county-maintained road — requires a right-of-way permit from the Marion County Office of the County Engineer. We build that step into any quote that touches county road frontage.

Our Process

  1. Document review. You send the survey and easement language; we confirm corridor location and width before quoting.
  2. Locates and flags. Sunshine 811 for existing utilities, corridor edges flagged on the ground.
  3. Clear and grub to the specified width — mulch-in-place where the corridor just needs to be open, full grubbing where a road base is going in.
  4. Stabilize. Sandy corridors on any grade get water bars or swale shaping so the first summer storm doesn't cut a gully down your new road line. With Ocala's ~51 inches of annual rain arriving mostly in hard afternoon bursts, an unstabilized sand corridor erodes in one season.
  5. Walk-through against the survey.

What Right-of-Way Clearing Costs

  • Mulched corridor (open it up, roots stay): roughly $2–$4 per linear foot at a typical 15–20 ft width
  • Grubbed corridor ready for road base: roughly $5–$12 per linear foot depending on width and tree density
  • Survey line cutting: usually priced per day — a light machine opens most boundary lines at a fraction of surveyor hourly rates

Long corridors price better per foot than short ones, and pine corridors price better than oak. A quarter-mile access easement through typical Marion County pine flatwoods usually lands in the $4,000–$12,000 range from first cut to drivable grade, before limerock.

Keeping the Corridor Open

A cleared corridor in Marion County is not a permanent condition — it's a maintained one. Our growing season closes an unmaintained mulched corridor in three to five years, faster along the sunny edges where blackberry and myrtle sprint. Re-clearing an established corridor costs a fraction of the first cut (typically 30–50%), because the stumps are gone and the machine just mows regrowth. Many easement holders put us on an every-two-to-three-year rotation and never face the full-price reclear again. If you share the easement with other parcel owners, a standing maintenance split is also far easier to negotiate than a surprise five-figure reclear.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I clear a deeded easement to my property myself?

A valid access easement generally gives you the right to make the corridor usable, but the specifics live in the recorded easement language. We clear strictly to the documented corridor and width — cutting outside it is trespass, and Florida law allows multiplied damages for wrongfully cut trees. If your deed is vague about width, resolve that before clearing.

How wide should I clear an access road corridor?

Twelve feet is the drivable minimum, but 16–20 feet is the practical standard — it accommodates fire apparatus, concrete trucks, and well-drilling rigs that a back parcel will eventually need. Utility companies specify their own corridor widths, and we clear to their spec so the corridor passes their inspection.

Do I need a permit to clear a right-of-way in Marion County?

On private upland easements, usually no special permit beyond normal clearing rules. But any work touching a county-maintained road — a new driveway connection or culvert — requires a right-of-way permit from the Marion County Office of the County Engineer, and wetland crossings need water management district review. We identify which applies before quoting.

How much does it cost to clear an access easement?

Mulched corridors run roughly $2–$4 per linear foot at typical width; fully grubbed corridors ready for road base run $5–$12 per linear foot. A quarter-mile easement through Marion County pine flatwoods usually lands between $4,000 and $12,000 from first cut to drivable grade, before limerock surfacing.

Will a cleared sand corridor wash out in the rain?

An unstabilized corridor cut through our sandy soil will erode in a single summer storm season — Ocala gets about 51 inches of rain a year, mostly in hard afternoon downpours. That's why our corridor work includes water bars or swale shaping on any grade, and why limerock capping is worth it on corridors you'll drive year-round.
