Land Grading & Leveling in Ocala, FL

Standing water against the house after every summer storm. A yard that slopes toward the slab instead of away from it. A barn pad that was never level and a gate that drags proof of it. Grading problems in Marion County nearly always trace to one root cause: the dirt was never shaped to move Florida's roughly 51 inches of annual rain — most of which arrives in violent June-to-September afternoon bursts that find every low spot you own.

Land grading is the fix: cutting high ground, filling low ground, and compacting the result into slopes that send water where it should go and surfaces that stay where you put them.

Grading Work We Do

  • Yard regrading and drainage correction — re-establishing positive fall away from foundations (the standard target: about 6 inches of drop in the first 10 feet)
  • Rough grading after clearing or construction — shaping raw or torn-up ground into usable land
  • Finish grading — the smooth final surface ready for sod, seed, or gravel
  • Laser-leveled pads — sheds, barns, garages, and slabs graded to tight tolerance
  • Horse arenas and round pens — Marion County specialty work: consistent grade with a 1–2% crown so an arena sheds storm water instead of holding it
  • Swales and berms — shaping the ground itself to intercept and route runoff
  • Driveway grading and re-crowning — see also driveway installation

Grading Sandy Ground Is Its Own Skill

Most of the Ocala area sits on deep Candler and Astatula fine sands. That sand is why our drainage problems look different from the rest of the country's: water rarely ponds for days here — it either soaks in fast or it moves fast, cutting channels and undermining edges as it goes. Grading for this ground means three things done right:

  • Compaction, always. Loose-dumped sand settles under its own weight and the first heavy rain. Every fill lift we place gets compacted, because a pad that's "level today" and 2 inches low next spring wasn't graded — it was piled.
  • Protect the surface. Bare graded sand erodes in one storm. We finish grades ready for immediate sod, seed, or mulch, and we'll tell you plainly: don't let a freshly graded yard sit bare through a wet season.
  • Respect the karst. Marion County's limestone bedrock means localized soft spots and, rarely, sinkhole activity. Concentrating roof and yard runoff into one unlined low spot is asking the ground a question you might not like the answer to. Good grading spreads and routes water deliberately.

Our Process

  1. Walk the property in the problem's own terms — ideally we see it after rain, or you show us phone video of where the water goes.
  2. Shoot grades. Laser level or rotary transit, real numbers, so the plan is math instead of eyeball.
  3. Written quote: cut/fill plan, any imported fill priced by the load, timeline.
  4. Cut, fill, and compact to the plan.
  5. Water-check walk-through. On drainage jobs we verify flow direction with a level before we call it done — and you watch.

What Grading Costs in the Ocala Area

  • Yard regrading / drainage correction: most residential jobs run $1,500–$6,000 depending on area and cut depth
  • Shed and garage pads: roughly $800–$2,500 laser-leveled and compacted
  • Barn pads and arenas: roughly $3,000–$15,000 by size — a 100x200 arena is a serious earthwork project
  • Imported fill: priced per load delivered; Marion County's local sand and limerock pits keep material costs reasonable compared to most of Florida

Every quote is a firm written number. If we open the ground and find something unquotable — buried debris is the classic on older Ocala properties — you get a written change order before anything continues, not a surprise invoice after.

After the Grade: Make It Stick

A grading job in Marion County isn't finished until the surface is protected, so plan the cover before the machines come. Sod is same-day protection and the right call on slopes and swale lines; bahia seed is cheaper but wants weeks of establishment — which in practice means seeding jobs are best scheduled at the front of the growing season, not the peak of storm season. If irrigation is going in, trench it before finish grade, not after. We coordinate grading dates with your sod or landscape contractor so the graded surface never sits exposed through a string of summer afternoons — the cheapest erosion control is a schedule that doesn't leave sand bare.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does land grading cost in Ocala?

Most residential regrading and drainage-correction jobs in Marion County run $1,500–$6,000, driven by square footage, cut depth, and whether fill must be imported. Laser-leveled shed pads run $800–$2,500, and barn pads or riding arenas run $3,000–$15,000 depending on size.

Will regrading fix standing water next to my foundation?

Usually yes — most foundation-water complaints in the Ocala area are grading problems: backfill that settled around the slab or landscaping built up above it. The standard fix re-establishes about 6 inches of fall in the first 10 feet away from the house. Where roof-water volume is the real culprit, gutters piped to daylight get added.

How is grading priced — by the hour or by the job?

We quote grading as a firm written job price based on shot grades and a cut/fill plan, with imported fill priced per delivered load. If something unquotable turns up underground — buried debris is the classic on older properties — you get a written change order before work continues.

Does freshly graded ground need grass right away?

In Marion County, yes. Our bare fine sand erodes in a single hard storm, and the summer season brings many. Grades should be sodded, seeded, or mulched promptly — we finish surfaces ready for immediate cover and will tell you plainly not to let a graded yard sit bare through the wet season.

Do I need a permit to regrade my yard in Marion County?

Routine residential regrading in unincorporated Marion County generally doesn't require a permit. Exceptions include grading that redirects drainage onto neighboring property (which Florida law prohibits), work within drainage easements, and grading that's part of permitted construction. We design grades so runoff is handled on your side of the line.

Can you grade a horse arena?

Yes — arena grading is a Marion County specialty of ours. A proper arena carries a consistent 1–2% crown or slope so it sheds storm water instead of ponding, with the base compacted before footing material goes down. A 100x200 arena is a genuine earthwork project and typically prices in the $3,000–$15,000 range depending on cut and fill.
