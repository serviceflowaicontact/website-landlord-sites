Driveway Installation & Repair in Ocala, FL

A rural driveway in Marion County lives a hard life: 51 inches of rain a year trying to wash it, fine sand underneath trying to swallow it, and everything from horse trailers to concrete trucks driving it. The drives that survive have three things in common — a compacted base, a crown that sheds water, and drainage that gives the water somewhere to go. The drives that fail were "a load of rock spread on the sand," and Marion County is full of those too.

Driveway Work We Do

  • New limerock driveways — the Marion County standard: locally quarried limerock, placed on compacted subgrade, crowned and rolled. It packs into a hard, drivable crust that handles Florida weather better than loose gravel
  • Gravel and crushed-asphalt (millings) drives — priced options with honest trade-offs; millings pack hard and shed water well when compacted properly in the heat
  • Long farm and acreage drives — quarter-mile runs from the county road to the homesite, common on parcels around Citra, Anthony, Fort McCoy, and out toward Dunnellon
  • Driveway repair and regrading — filling washboard and potholes properly (cut, re-crown, re-compact — not just filling holes that reopen in a month)
  • Culvert installation at the road — where your drive crosses the county ditch line, a culvert set to grade is required, and connections to county-maintained roads go through Marion County right-of-way permitting; we handle that step
  • Construction access drives — temporary rock access so the concrete trucks can reach your build site all through the wet season

Why the Base Is Everything in Our Sand

Ocala-area subgrade is fine sand — Candler, Astatula, Arredondo series depending on where you are. Rock spread straight on loose sand disappears into it: traffic pumps the sand up, the rock punches down, and in a year you own a sandy strip with occasional rocks. Built right, the same materials last a decade or more:

  1. Shape and compact the subgrade first. The sand itself gets graded to a crown and rolled tight.
  2. Place base rock in lifts and compact each lift. For most residential drives that's 4–6 inches of limerock; heavy-use farm drives get more.
  3. Crown the finished surface 2–4% so rain runs off the sides instead of down the wheel tracks — on our summer downpours, a flat drive becomes a stream bed.
  4. Handle the water at the edges: shallow side swales on long drives, a culvert wherever the drive crosses a flow line.

What Driveways Cost in Marion County

  • Typical residential limerock drive (60–150 ft): roughly $1,500–$4,500 installed with compacted base and crown
  • Long acreage drives: roughly $10–$20 per linear foot at standard 10–12 ft width, driven by rock depth and any clearing needed — a quarter-mile drive typically lands $15,000–$25,000 done right
  • Culvert at the road: roughly $1,200–$3,500 including pipe, set to grade, permit handling where county frontage requires it
  • Regrade and re-rock of an existing drive: often $800–$2,500 — much cheaper than new because the base already exists

One genuine local advantage: limerock is quarried in and around Marion County, so material trucking — the biggest cost in rural driveway work — stays short and prices stay below what most of Florida pays for the same drive.

Keeping a Limerock Drive Alive

The maintenance schedule for a properly built drive is refreshingly short. Once a year or so — ideally before storm season — the drive should be regraded to restore the crown and redistribute rock from the edges back into the wheel paths; every several years it takes a fresh topping load. That annual regrade is a quick machine visit, often $300–$600 on a typical residential drive, and it postpones the far larger cost of rebuilding a drive that's been let go. We put drives on a maintenance rotation for owners who'd rather never think about it again — which, on a quarter-mile farm drive, is most owners.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a gravel or limerock driveway cost in Ocala?

A typical residential limerock drive of 60–150 feet runs $1,500–$4,500 installed with a compacted base and crown. Long acreage drives run roughly $10–$20 per linear foot, so a quarter-mile drive done right typically lands between $15,000 and $25,000. Locally quarried limerock keeps Marion County prices below most of Florida.

What's the best driveway material for Marion County?

Limerock is the local standard: it's quarried nearby, packs into a hard drivable crust, and repairs cheaply. Asphalt millings pack even harder and shed water well but cost more. Loose decorative gravel migrates the worst on our sand — if you want the look, put it as a top course over a compacted limerock base.

Why does my driveway wash out every summer?

Almost always because it has no crown, no side drainage, or it sits in a natural flow path — and Ocala's summer storms exploit all three. The fix is a regrade to a 2–4% crown plus swales or a culvert where water crosses, usually a one-day job that ends the annual washout.

Do I need a permit to put in a driveway in Marion County?

Not for the drive on your own property, but connecting to a county-maintained road — including setting the culvert through the county ditch line — requires a right-of-way permit through the Marion County Office of the County Engineer. We handle that step as part of any new road-connection quote.

Why can't you just spread rock on the sand?

Because rock placed on loose fine sand punches down while traffic pumps the sand up, and within a year the rock has effectively disappeared. A lasting drive requires the sand subgrade shaped and compacted first, then base rock placed and compacted in lifts, then a crowned surface — same materials, completely different lifespan.
