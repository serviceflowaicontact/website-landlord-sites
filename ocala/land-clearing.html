Land Clearing in Ocala, FL

Buy five acres off US 301 near Citra or a wooded parcel outside Dunnellon and the listing photos always undersell one thing: how much is actually growing on it. Laurel oak, water oak, slash pine, and a waist-high tangle of saw palmetto and grapevine — typical Marion County cover, and none of it moves itself. Land clearing is the job of taking a parcel from that state to whatever state you need: fully cleared for a build, selectively cleared for pasture, or opened up enough to finally see your own property lines.

What Land Clearing Includes

Every job is scoped in writing, but a full clear in the Ocala area typically covers:

  • Tree removal — cutting and processing everything marked to go, from pine saplings to mature oaks
  • Grubbing — digging out stumps and root balls, not just cutting flush and leaving a problem under your future pad
  • Underbrush and palmetto removal — palmetto root mats are the stubborn part of most Marion County clears; they come out or they grow back
  • Debris handling — hauling off, burning where permitted, or mulching on site, priced per your preference
  • Rough grading — knocking down the holes and ruts so the parcel is drivable and mowable when we leave

If you only need the understory gone and want the canopy kept, that's a different (cheaper) job — see our brush removal and forestry mulching pages.

How We Clear a Parcel, Step by Step

  1. Walk it with you. We flag keep-trees with tape, note wet spots and grade changes, and check access for equipment.
  2. Check the rules for your parcel. Marion County's Land Development Code protects certain trees, and clearing tied to a building permit gets reviewed with that permit. Parcels with low, wet areas may touch water management district jurisdiction — St. Johns River WMD on the east side of the county, Southwest Florida WMD out toward Dunnellon. And in sandy uplands, we look for gopher tortoise burrows before any machine moves, because tortoises are protected statewide and relocation requires an FWC permit. We flag all of this in the quote, not after.
  3. Written quote. Per-acre or whole-job price, debris plan, timeline.
  4. Clear, grub, and process debris per the plan.
  5. Final walk-through before we load the machines.

What Land Clearing Costs in Marion County

Honest ranges, because "call for pricing" wastes everyone's time:

  • Light cover (brush, palmetto, scattered small trees): roughly $1,500–$3,000 per acre
  • Moderately wooded (mixed pine and oak, typical of most parcels between Ocala and Anthony): roughly $3,000–$5,000 per acre
  • Heavily wooded with grubbing and haul-off: $5,000+ per acre, driven mostly by stump count and debris volume

Marion County clearing tends to run cheaper than clay-country clearing further north because our deep Candler and Astatula fine sands dig easily and machines rarely bog down — outside of the June-to-September storm season, when we sometimes wait a day for the ground to firm up. Haul-off is often the single biggest line item, which is why keeping mulch or burn-processing on site (where allowed) can cut the total meaningfully.

Why Clearing Is Booming Here

Ocala has topped national metro growth rankings for several years, and it shows in the work: new homesites around the World Equestrian Center and NW 80th Avenue, infill lots finally getting built in Marion Oaks and Silver Springs Shores, and buyers from out of state closing on raw acreage in Citra, Anthony, and Fort McCoy. If you're one of them, clearing is the first check you write — and the one that determines whether the site prep after it goes smoothly.

When to Schedule Clearing

Dry season — roughly October through May — is prime clearing time in Marion County: the sand is firm, burn authorizations are easier to work with outside drought alerts, and machines lose fewer days to weather. Summer clearing is entirely doable, but budget a rain-delay day or two and expect debris burning to be more restricted. If your build has a hard start date, get the clearing quoted a month or two ahead; the same growth boom that's filling Marion Oaks with new houses keeps every clearing crew in the county busy, and the good ones book out.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to clear 1 acre in Ocala?

For a typical moderately wooded acre in Marion County, budget $3,000–$5,000 including grubbing and debris handling. Light palmetto-and-brush acres can run $1,500–$3,000, while dense oak hammocks with heavy grubbing and haul-off can exceed $5,000. An exact number requires a free site walk.

Do I need a permit to clear my land in Marion County?

For most residential parcels, clearing underbrush and ordinary trees does not require a standalone county permit. However, Marion County's tree protection rules can apply to large specimen trees, clearing tied to new construction is reviewed with the building permit, and wetland areas fall under St. Johns River or Southwest Florida water management district rules. We verify what applies to your parcel before quoting.

What happens to the debris after land clearing?

You choose from three priced options: haul-off to a legal disposal site, on-site burning where county and state fire rules allow, or grinding the debris into mulch that stays on the property. Haul-off is usually the largest single cost in a clearing job, so keeping material on site often saves real money.

How long does land clearing take?

A quarter-acre residential lot is usually done in a day. One to five wooded acres typically takes two days to a week depending on tree density and the debris plan. Every written quote includes a working-day estimate.

What about gopher tortoises on my property?

Gopher tortoises are common in Marion County's dry sandy uplands and are protected by Florida law — clearing over an active burrow requires an FWC relocation permit first. We check for burrows during the free estimate walk so it never stalls your job midway.

Will clearing leave ruts and holes?

No — rough grading is part of our standard clearing scope, so the parcel is left mowable and drivable. If you need a compacted, build-ready pad, that is a separate site preparation service we also provide.
