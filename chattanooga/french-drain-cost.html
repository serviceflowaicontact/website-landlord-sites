What a French Drain Costs in Chattanooga, TN (2026)

Short answer: in 2026, an exterior French drain in Chattanooga and Hamilton County typically runs $25–$50 per linear foot installed, and most complete yard systems land between $2,500 and $6,000. A typical project — an 80–120 foot run intercepting water on the uphill side of a house and carrying it to daylight — budgets $3,000–$5,000 including gravel, fabric-wrapped pipe and surface restoration.

2026 Price Ranges — Hamilton County

  • Shallow curtain drain (surface-water interception, 12–18" deep): $10–$25 / linear foot

  • Deep or foundation-adjacent drain (footing depth, waterproofing coordination): $50–$100+ / linear foot

What Actually Drives the Price

The three big variables here are depth in clay, discharge distance, and access.

  • Depth and the clay layer. Chattanooga red clay percolates slowly, so an effective drain sits deeper and carries a bigger gravel envelope than the same drain in sandy soil. Digging 30" of clay costs more than 16" of loam — this is the honest reason local quotes beat national averages.
  • Where the water can go. A drain is only as good as its exit. A lot that falls away to daylight is the cheap case; a flat or bowl-shaped yard may need a dry well or pump basin, which adds $500–$2,000.
  • Access and obstacles. Fences, mature roots, tight side yards, buried utilities and hardscape crossings all add hand-digging hours. A machine-accessible run is always cheaper per foot.
  • Restoration. Trenching through established sod, beds or gravel costs more to leave looking finished than an open stretch of lawn.

How to Keep Your Drainage Bill Down

  • Fix gutters and grading first. Some "French drain problems" are really a $300 downspout-and-regrade problem. The free assessment tells you which — before you spend thousands. See regrading.
  • Bundle the dig. Burying downspouts, adding a channel drain, or running conduit while the trench is open costs a fraction of doing each separately.
  • Schedule in a dry stretch. Saturated clay slows digging and wrecks lawns; dry-season work is faster and restores cleaner.
  • Keep the discharge on your property plan. Draining onto a neighbor creates a dispute and sometimes a redo — build the exit right the first time.

Why Cheap Drains Fail Here

The most common failure we see is a skinny trench with bare corrugated pipe and a scoop of gravel — in red clay it silts shut in a season or two. A drain built for this soil wraps the entire gravel column in non-woven filter fabric, uses rigid perforated pipe with cleanouts, and slopes continuously to a real exit. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a French drain cost in Chattanooga?

Most exterior French drains in Chattanooga run $25–$50 per linear foot installed in 2026, with complete yard systems typically totaling $2,500–$6,000 depending on length, depth in clay, and where the water can discharge.

How many feet of French drain do I need?

Most residential fixes need 40–120 feet — usually less than homeowners expect. The drain only has to intercept the path water actually travels, which is why an on-site assessment beats guessing from a satellite photo.

Is a French drain worth it for a wet basement or crawlspace?

If water is reaching the foundation from outside, an exterior French drain is usually the highest-value fix because it stops the water before the wall. Interior systems manage water that's already inside; exterior systems keep it out. Many Chattanooga homes on clay need the exterior fix first.

Why are French drains more expensive in clay soil?

Red clay drains slowly, so the trench must be deeper, the gravel envelope larger, and the whole column wrapped in filter fabric to keep clay fines from clogging it. That's more digging, more stone and more fabric per foot — but it's the difference between a 20-year drain and a 2-year one.

Do I need a permit for a French drain in Chattanooga?

Typical residential yard drains don't require a permit, but any dig legally requires a free Tennessee 811 utility locate first — we call it in on every job. Work that ties into storm sewers or changes discharge onto other properties can involve city stormwater rules; we flag that in the assessment when it applies.
