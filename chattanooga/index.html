Why Chattanooga Yards Flood

Three local factors gang up on homeowners here:

  • Red clay subsoil. Once the top few inches of topsoil saturate, our clay passes water at a crawl. Rain that would disappear into sandy soil in an hour sits on a Chattanooga lawn for days.
  • Ridge-and-valley terrain. Homes below Signal Mountain, Lookout Mountain, and Missionary Ridge receive runoff from every lot — and every acre of rock — above them. Valley-floor neighborhoods like East Ridge and Brainerd collect what the slopes shed.
  • Heavy rainfall, delivered hard. About 55 inches a year, much of it in intense summer thunderstorms. The August 2025 flash floods that shut down parts of I-24 were an extreme case of what smaller storms do to yards every month.

A drainage fix that ignores any one of those factors fails. That's why we start every job with a water-flow assessment: where the water comes from, where it sits, and where it can legally and safely go.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my yard flood every time it rains in Chattanooga?

Almost always it's the combination of red clay soil and slope. Chattanooga's clay subsoil absorbs water very slowly, so once it saturates, everything else runs across the surface and collects at the lowest point — often a backyard or a spot beside the foundation. If your lot sits below a ridge or a neighbor's property, you're also receiving their runoff. A drainage assessment identifies which of those is driving your specific problem.

Who do I call for standing water in my yard?

A drainage contractor — not a plumber, and usually not a general landscaper. Plumbers handle water inside the pipes of your house; drainage contractors handle rainwater on and under your property. We design and install french drains, catch basins, regrading, and downspout systems, which are the tools that actually eliminate standing water.

How much does drainage work cost in Chattanooga?

Small fixes like burying downspout lines often run $800–$2,500. A typical french drain installation runs $2,500–$8,000 depending on length and depth. Full yard systems with catch basins and multiple runs range $3,000–$12,000. Chattanooga's clay makes digging slower than in sandy markets, which is reflected in local pricing — but every job gets a firm written quote before work starts.

Do I need a permit for drainage work at my house?

Routine residential drainage work — french drains, downspout burial, minor regrading — generally doesn't require a city permit. Rules do apply to where the water goes: you can't discharge concentrated flow onto a neighbor's property, and connecting to the city storm system requires approval. We handle routing legally on every job, and the City of Chattanooga's RainSmart program even offers incentives for some rain-capture practices.
