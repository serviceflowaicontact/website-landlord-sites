Yard Drainage Systems in Chattanooga, TN

Some yards need one french drain. Others need a system: catch basins where water pools, channel drains across the driveway, buried solid pipe tying it all together, and a designed discharge point that can handle a Chattanooga thunderstorm. We design and install complete yard drainage systems — surface and subsurface working as one.

What a Yard Drainage System Includes

Every system is designed from your lot's actual water pattern, but the toolbox looks like this:

  • Catch basins. Grated inlets set at the low points where water collects, connected to buried pipe. The fix for the puddle that shows up in the same spot every storm.
  • Channel drains. Long, narrow grates across driveways, patios, and walkways that intercept sheeting water before it reaches the garage or the door.
  • Solid buried pipe. The transport network. Unlike a french drain's perforated pipe, solid pipe moves collected water fast and can't be infiltrated by clay silt.
  • French drain runs. Where subsurface water needs collecting — see French drain installation for how those are built.
  • Pop-up emitters, daylight outlets, or dry wells. Every gallon collected has to go somewhere legal and safe. Designing the discharge is half the job.

Signs You Need a System, Not a Single Drain

  • Water problems in more than one area of the yard
  • Water ponding on hardscape — driveway, patio, pool deck
  • Runoff arriving from uphill neighbors or a ridge behind the property
  • Downspouts, yard puddles, and a wet crawl space all at once
  • Previous single-drain fixes that helped one spot and moved the problem to another

That last one is common. Drainage is a whole-lot question: fix the low spot without handling the water source and you've just relocated the pond. A system design starts from where the water originates — roof, slope, neighbor, saturated soil — and handles it end to end.

Designed for Chattanooga Conditions

Valley lots collect everything

Chattanooga's ridge-and-valley layout means neighborhoods like East Ridge, Brainerd, and parts of Red Bank sit on valley floors that receive runoff from the slopes around them. East Ridge in particular is famously flat and low — water that reaches a lot there has nowhere to go without help. Systems on valley lots emphasize collection capacity and engineered discharge, because gravity gives you very little for free.

Slope lots shed water violently

On Signal Mountain, Lookout Valley hillsides, and ridge-side streets, the problem is the opposite: water arrives fast and concentrated. There, systems emphasize interception — catching flow at the top of the property with channel drains, swales, and interceptor french drains before it can build speed.

Clay soil means surface solutions matter more

Because our red clay accepts water so slowly, a higher share of every storm runs across the surface here than in most of the country. That's why pure french-drain thinking under-serves many Chattanooga yards: you need surface capture — basins, channels, swales and dry creek beds — to handle the water that never soaks in at all.

Our Design-and-Install Process

  1. Free water-flow assessment. We map where water enters, travels, pools, and exits. If you have photos or video from a recent storm, they're gold.
  2. System design and firm quote. A sketch of the layout — inlets, pipe runs, discharge — with a written price and timeline.
  3. Utility locates. Called in before any digging, every job.
  4. Installation. Trenching, basin setting, pipe laid to laser-checked grade, connections sealed, backfill compacted in lifts so nothing settles into a trench-line dip next spring.
  5. Flow test and walkthrough. We run water through every inlet and show you the system working before we call it done.

What Yard Drainage Systems Cost in Chattanooga

Multi-component systems in the Chattanooga area typically run $3,000–$12,000, driven by:

  • Number of collection points. Each catch basin or channel drain adds excavation, materials, and connections.
  • Total pipe footage. Buried solid pipe generally runs $15–$30 per foot installed here — clay trenching is slower than the digging most national cost guides assume.
  • Discharge engineering. A gravity daylight outlet is cheapest; dry wells or long routing runs add cost.
  • Hardscape crossings. Cutting and restoring concrete or pavers for a channel drain costs more than open-lawn trenching.

A single catch basin tied to an existing outlet can be as little as $800–$1,500. A full-perimeter system on a problem lot can exceed the top of the range. Either way you'll have a firm number in writing before we dig.

One Note on City Stormwater Rules

Chattanooga regulates stormwater under its Water Quality program — it's why a water quality fee appears on city bills. For homeowners, the practical rules are simple: don't discharge concentrated flow onto neighboring property, and get approval before tying into public storm infrastructure. We design every system to comply, and the city's RainSmart program offers incentives for rain gardens and capture practices we can fold into a design if you want them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does water stand in my Chattanooga yard for days after it rains?

Red clay is the usual culprit. Chattanooga's clay subsoil percolates water extremely slowly, so once it saturates — which happens fast in a 53-inch-a-year rainfall climate — additional water has nowhere to go but the surface. If your lot is also flat or sits below surrounding grades, as much of East Ridge and Brainerd does, the water collects and simply waits to evaporate. A drainage system gives it an actual exit.

What's the difference between a french drain and a yard drainage system?

A french drain is one component: a gravel-and-perforated-pipe trench that collects water moving through the soil. A yard drainage system is the whole solution — catch basins and channel drains for surface water, solid pipe to transport it, french drain runs where subsurface collection is needed, and a designed discharge point. Many Chattanooga yards need the system because clay soil sends so much water across the surface, where a french drain alone can't catch it.

How much does a full yard drainage system cost?

In the Chattanooga area, most multi-component systems run $3,000–$12,000 depending on the number of catch basins, total pipe footage, and how the discharge is engineered. A single basin tied to an existing line might be under $1,500, while a full-perimeter system on a difficult lot can go higher. Every job gets a free assessment and a firm written price first.

Can you fix water coming from my neighbor's yard?

Yes — this is one of our most common calls, especially on sloped streets where lots stack downhill. The standard fix is an interceptor drain or swale along the shared boundary that catches the flow before it crosses your lawn and routes it to a legal discharge point. What we can't do is send it back at them: city stormwater rules prohibit discharging concentrated flow onto neighboring property, so the design has to route water responsibly.

How long does a drainage system installation take?

Most residential systems go in over two to four days depending on pipe footage, the number of inlets, and whether we're cutting any concrete. Weather matters too — we won't fine-grade or compact backfill in soup. You'll get a schedule with your quote and a call if rain moves it.
