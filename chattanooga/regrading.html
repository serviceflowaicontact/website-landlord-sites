Yard Grading & Regrading in Chattanooga, TN

Every drainage fix works with gravity or fights it. When the ground itself slopes the wrong way — toward the house, into a low corner, off a neighbor's lot and straight at your patio — pipes and drains are treating symptoms. Regrading treats the cause: we reshape the ground so water runs where it should, permanently, with no parts to clog or pumps to fail.

What Regrading Fixes

  • Negative grade at the foundation. Soil that slopes toward the house — the most damaging drainage defect a home can have, and one of the most common we find on Chattanooga lots that have settled for decades.
  • Low spots and birdbaths. Depressions where water ponds and grass drowns.
  • Settled trenches and old excavation lines. Utility trenches and old work that sank into channels that now collect water.
  • Side-yard swales that quit working. Many lots were built with a shallow drainage swale between houses; decades of mulch, fill, and fencing often erase it.
  • Backyards that hold water wall-to-wall. Sometimes the whole plane needs re-establishing with fall toward a designed exit path.

The Standard We Grade To

Around a foundation, the target is at least a 5% fall for the first 10 feet — about 6 inches of drop. Across open lawn, a 1–2% fall keeps water moving without feeling sloped underfoot. We shoot everything with a laser level; eyeballing grade is how yards got this way in the first place.

Grading in Chattanooga Terrain Is Its Own Skill

Ridge-and-valley lots rarely offer a flat start

Chattanooga homes sit on benched hillsides, valley floors, and everything between. On slope lots — Signal Mountain, the flanks of Missionary Ridge, hillside streets in Red Bank — regrading is often about managing water across the lot: building swales and berms that intercept downhill flow and steer it around the house rather than letting it arrive at the back door. On valley-floor lots in East Ridge or Brainerd, the challenge is opposite: creating fall where nature provided almost none, which takes careful laser work because there are only a few inches of elevation to spend.

Red clay cuts both ways

Clay is miserable to dig but excellent to shape: graded and compacted properly, it holds its form and sheds water beautifully — clay's low permeability becomes an asset when the slope is right. We compact fill in lifts so the new grade doesn't settle back into the old problem, and we finish with topsoil so grass can actually root.

Keep soil away from siding and weep holes

Raising grade against a house has rules: soil stays several inches below siding, brick weep holes stay open, and crawl-space vents stay clear. Fixing negative grade sometimes means cutting soil away and rebuilding the slope lower — more work than just dumping fill, and the reason quick-fix grading jobs often create moisture problems worse than the puddle they buried.

What Regrading Costs in Chattanooga

Typical local ranges:

  • Foundation perimeter regrade (correcting negative grade around the house): $1,500–$4,000
  • Section regrade (a backyard, a side yard, a settled area with fill, compaction, and topsoil): $2,000–$5,500
  • Full-yard regrade with swale construction and lawn restoration: $4,000–$10,000+

Cost drivers are machine access (gates, slopes, trees), how much material must be moved or imported, haul-off of surplus clay, and surface restoration — seed is cheaper than sod. Slope lots cost more per square foot than flat ones: equipment works slower across grade and often needs multiple passes to build stable benches. As always, the price is firm and in writing before the first bucket of dirt moves.

Grading and Drains: Better Together

Regrading handles surface water; it doesn't stop subsurface water already moving through soil, and some lots simply have nowhere on the surface to send flow. That's when we combine grading with a french drain along the interception line, a dry creek bed or swale as the designed exit path, or buried downspout lines so roof water doesn't re-flood the new grade. The assessment tells us the right mix — and grading first often shrinks the drain system you need, saving money overall.

Our Process

  1. Free assessment with laser grades. We measure actual fall around the foundation and across problem areas — numbers, not guesses.
  2. Written plan and firm price. Where we cut, where we fill, where the water will go, what the finished surface will be.
  3. Utility locates, then earthwork. Compact machines sized to your access; hand work near foundations, roots, and utilities.
  4. Compaction in lifts, topsoil, restoration. Seed and straw or sod, your choice.
  5. Water test. First good rain — or a hose test — confirms flow goes where the plan said. If anything ponds, we come back and fix it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to regrade a yard in Chattanooga?

Foundation-perimeter corrections typically run $1,500–$4,000, section regrades $2,000–$5,500, and full-yard regrading with swales and lawn restoration $4,000–$10,000 or more. Machine access, how much clay must be moved or hauled off, and slope steepness drive the price — hillside lots cost more per square foot than flat ones. We quote firm after a free laser-measured assessment.

How can I tell if my yard slopes toward my house?

Watch during rain: water running toward the foundation, pooling against it, or a damp ring of soil at the base of the wall are the giveaways. Indoors, chronic crawl space moisture or basement dampness on one side of the house points the same direction. We measure it precisely with a laser level — the standard is about 6 inches of fall in the first 10 feet, and many settled Chattanooga lots have half that or actual negative grade.

Can you fix a low spot without regrading the whole yard?

Usually, yes. An isolated low spot can often be filled, compacted, and blended into surrounding grade in a day, or fitted with a catch basin if raising it would push water somewhere worse. The key is checking where the displaced water will go before filling — a low spot is sometimes the only thing protecting your foundation, and we'd rather tell you that than take a quick job.

Will regrading kill my grass?

The work area gets disturbed — there's no honest way around it — but we finish every regrade with topsoil and either seed-and-straw or sod. Fescue establishes well here in fall and early spring, so we'll time seeding accordingly when the schedule allows. Within a season, a properly restored regrade looks better than the drowned, patchy grass it replaced, because grass roots can finally breathe.

Do I need a permit to regrade my yard in Chattanooga?

Typical residential regrading doesn't require a permit, but the city's stormwater ordinance still applies to results: you can't redirect concentrated runoff onto neighboring property, and larger land disturbance triggers city requirements. Near creeks and in mapped floodplain areas — parts of East Ridge and Brainerd, for example — additional rules can apply to adding fill. We design within the rules and flag anything on your lot that needs a closer look.
